Founder, Morenita Technology
Amelia Arabe
Amelia Arabe is an engineer, cellist and painter who founded Morenita Technology in 2025 to build technology for people. She studied computer and electrical engineering at Loyola University Maryland and works from Los Angeles on biomedical devices, materials research and the software that holds them together. She believes the best work happens when the people making it feel safe to be fully themselves.
Where Morenita began.

Amelia grew up in Southern California in a house that refused to pick one lane for her.

Her parents expected a sharp mind, a strong body and a creative life to travel together, so schoolwork always ran beside a sport and an instrument. Piano and dance became cello and orchestra. Math pulled her toward engineering, and the music and the painting never let go. She learned early that rigor and beauty were never opposites.

She carried that conviction into engineering school and saw what it cost to hold it there. Brilliant people split themselves across a dozen platforms and personas just to be seen. Most of the technology around them was built to hold attention rather than to build anything real. And she rarely heard a voice like her own explaining hard technical ideas to the girls who needed to hear one.

The projects that followed gave her direction. A university capstone team set out to build a voice-activated hospital bed, and that work grew into MAXINE, an open-source and modular recovery companion. In a campus arboretum she built ARNOLD, a rover that hunts the spotted lanternfly, and in field validation it helped protect 2,200 trees. Every project taught the same lesson. The machines mattered, and the people around them and the way they worked together mattered more.

In 2025 she founded Morenita Technology to bring all of it under one roof. The studio designs and builds modular hardware, software and materials, first for its own projects and then as open, adaptable products that anyone can build on.

What Morenita Technology is.

A creative technology studio in Los Angeles, built to prove that technology can foster community and preserve charisma instead of draining them.

The studio works in modular tech, sustainable energy and functional fashion. Its teams stay small and specific, and the infrastructure underneath is built to carry whatever the studio grows into next.

Biomedical devices.
MAXINE is an open-source, modular system for tracking vital signs and supporting recovery. It is an engineering and research project, and it is not a cleared medical device.
Materials and energy.
Our research looks at photovoltaic textiles and natural fibers, so the things people wear and use can quietly make power of their own.
Field robotics.
ARNOLD began as a pest-management rover in an arboretum and shows how autonomy can protect living systems instead of straining them.
Stories and learning.
The archive and the newsletter explain how each thing gets made, in plain and respectful language, so more people can learn to make things too.
The north stars
01
Green energy for everyone.
Clean power should reach every community that needs it. The studio designs toward net-zero and shares what it learns along the way.
02
Education as liberation.
A hard idea explained with respect can change a life. Amelia writes, teaches and builds in public so that more people can do this work.
03
Technology that centers people.
Every device starts with the person who will use it, with a human in the loop and with accessibility from the first sketch.
04
Infrastructure for community.
Makers, artists and engineers do their best work side by side. The studio builds the quiet, steady structure that lets them.
05
A seat in endurance racing.
This one belongs to Amelia alone. The research in energy, materials and engineering all points down the same long road.
How Amelia leads.

She leads the way a cellist tunes an orchestra.

Infrastructure should never be the loud part. It sets the tempo and the tone so that everyone else can play. Amelia builds teams where people feel safe to explore themselves, their ambitions and each other, free of judgment and free of competition, because she believes beautiful things come from healthy human connection. She also believes the pursuit of knowledge and the pursuit of happiness belong together, and that a team built on both makes healthier futures.

Our mission
Build technology that gives people more room to be human.
Morenita Technology exists to build technology that fosters community and preserves charisma. We refuse the idea that engineering and art, rigor and beauty, or ambition and kindness have to compete. We build modular tech, sustainable energy and functional fashion that prove they belong together.
The world we decided not to accept.

Most technology is built to extract attention, not to build anything real.

It rewards the loudest voice. It splits brilliant, many-sided people across a dozen platforms and asks them to be one thing at a time. And it leaves the physical world, the one with hospital beds, power grids and the fabric on our backs, waiting for someone to care enough to rebuild it.

We decided to care. We decided that technology should hand people their time, their health, their power and their stories back. We decided the work would start now, small and specific, and that we would build it in the open so anyone could see how it is done and join in.

What we will not stop building toward
01
Power for everyone.
We design toward net-zero energy, and we share what we learn so that clean power reaches the communities that need it most.
02
Knowledge without gates.
Education is liberation. We explain hard ideas in plain, respectful language, and we open our work so that more people can build.
03
Technology that serves the person.
Every device and tool we make starts with a human in the loop and with accessibility from the first sketch.
04
Places and tools to build together.
We build the quiet, steady infrastructure that lets makers, artists and engineers do their best work side by side.
How we do it
Modular tech.
We build open, adaptable hardware and software that people can take apart, improve and make their own.
Sustainable energy.
We research solar power and photovoltaic textiles so that clean energy becomes part of how people live and work.
Functional fashion.
We design textiles and wearables where form and function arrive together, and where nothing beautiful is wasted.
What we believe
Synchronicity over speed.
The best work happens when the conditions for collaboration are right, not when things move fastest.
Art and engineering are the same discipline.
Tools built without beauty are incomplete. Beauty without function is decorative. We do not choose.
Technology should expand human capacity.
Not replace it, not simplify it, not optimize it. Expand it. Give people more room to be human.
Community before commerce.
Morenita Technology exists to serve a community first. Revenue is what makes that possible, not the point.
What we promise
We tell the truth about our work.
Research is called research. We never claim a product does what it has not been proven to do.
We open what we can.
Good tools should spread. Where we can, we publish how things are built so others can build further.
We protect the people who build with us.
Every collaborator gets credit, care and a place where they feel safe to explore.
We measure ourselves by people.
The question we ask of every project is whether the people it touches have more room to live, make and rest.
Join the work
If you believe technology should serve people, build with us.
Engineers, artists, researchers, writers and curious people are all welcome here. Read the stories, follow the work, and tell us what you want to make.
